## Pre-draft checklist

Run each item against the draft before running the gates.

**Attribution**
- [ ] A source already in `facts.md` does not mean the claim is sourced. Check that the ledger LINE records the part of the document the claim rests on. Beck's page asserted in three fields that the right practice differs by 3X phase; `[S:24]` cited the very post that says "Applying the approach from one phase to an idea in another phase kills ideas", but its ledger line had recorded only that the post credits no antecedent. The page was parked in round 3 for an unsourced claim whose source was already on file. `fact-ledger.ts` cannot catch this: it verifies that a field cites a line, never that the line supports the field. **Second sighting on 2026-09-22**, in a return-edge label: the planned Larson label credited the 1992 OOPSLA report with pricing the cost of code that no longer matches what its team understands, which is the 2009 talk `[S:4]` and not the report. Both facts sit on the same page's ledger and both are Cunningham's, which is what makes the swap invisible. A thinker with two canonical statements of one idea is where this check is easiest to skip.
- [ ] **When a claim is lowered on evidence in one field, grep for every field that carries it and lower them in the same pass.** Two runs. On Anderson, round 2 softened the solo credit in `reading` and `tagline` on `[S:39]` and `[S:14]`, `thirty_second_take` kept the hard version, and all five seats reached it a round later. On Cunningham the adjudicator struck "by name" from the Larson edge label and left "borrowing its shape" standing in `still_load_bearing_despite` and in `inclusion_rationale`, where `[S:27]` records that Larson credits no prior source at all. The claims map line for each field is the place to find the other carriers. Both sightings were found a round after the lowering, by a reader who had not made the edit.
- [ ] No frame is marked `original` without an antecedent search. Earlier uses found so far: Patrick Kua on the tech lead role, Evan Bottcher on the platform definition, Dan McKinley on boring technology.
- [ ] Every co-author of a coined term is named in `co_originators` and wherever the term appears in prose.
- [ ] A counterposition that never names the thinker is a `parallel` edge, not `critiques`.
- [ ] No compressed phrasing in the tagline, take, thesis, reading or extension presents the site's interpretation as the thinker's claim or intent ("as Forsgren built them" implied a design intent no source supports; "Hickey's argument asks why the rule stops at the log" put a security question in the mouth of someone who never discussed audit logs; on the append-dont-overwrite concept page, "Fowler compares the event log to a git history, where a leaked secret stays in every clone" let a relative clause hang the site's point on Fowler. Put the site's point in its own sentence).

**Grounding**
- [ ] `bun scripts/fact-ledger.ts <slug>` exits 0. Every frame attribution, co-originator, `critiques` or `antecedent` edge, book, `active_years` and `background` cites a verified `[S:n]` with a URL. A claim with no ledger line does not go on the page, whoever proposed it.
- [ ] Read the ledger's `warn unused` lines. A verified fact nothing cites is usually a claim a rewrite dropped, or a seat's assertion that research could not confirm.
- [ ] Read the gate warnings, as well as its exit code. `reading-gates.ts` fails on `reading` and `extension` and only warns on the other editorial fields, because a banned cadence is sometimes right there. A shipped `thesis` carried "not X but Y" until a review seat caught it by hand on 2026-09-17.
- [ ] Every citation supports its own sentence, and every clause of it. A sentence that fuses a cited point with an uncited one fails (Hickey's privacy-deletion clause rode on a limit about unfamiliarity). A Lens citation belongs on the sentence that makes the Lens argument, not the setup sentence before it (Komoroske, Woods, Fournier).
- [ ] Inference lives in `extension`. A `reading` sentence that draws a conclusion the sources do not state moves to `extension` or goes.
- [ ] Limits stay limits. Do not recast a limit as evidence the method works (Fowler's microservices retraction, Dalio's Bridgewater gap).
- [ ] Rewrites get re-audited. A revision that adds a new claim can add a new unsupported one ("compromised account" on Komoroske).
- [ ] **After an edit that changes a count, a name or a citation, read the sentence on either side of it.** The repair is usually correct and the sentence beside it usually still describes the state before the repair. Four instances across the two pages of the 2026-09-22 batch, all in text round 2 had just edited. On Cunningham, fixing the coinage count in `reading` left "A third carries his name and was never his" with no referent, and adding `[S:27]` under the Larson edge left "by name" standing beside a ledger line recording that Larson credits nobody. On Humble, a repair to `limits[3]` left "none of them addresses" reaching past its antecedent. **Both sightings come from one batch and one adjudicated round**, so the promotion rests on a narrower base than usual; send it back to "Caught once" if the next two runs do not reproduce it.
- [ ] Before cutting an unledgered claim, check what else on the page depends on it. On Anderson both unledgered claims were true and both were in the source's own text, so sourcing them was cheaper than cutting, and cutting the aviation comparison would have left a `strength: primary` neighbour edge standing on nothing.
- [ ] Chronology holds. A book cannot draw on a job the author took later (Fournier's 2017 book and 2022 role).
- [ ] Superlatives and "usually" claims need a source ("the largest attack in decades," "usually where authority changes").

**Argument**
- [ ] The extension does not walk into a failure mode the page's own limits name (proposing a new metric right after citing Goodhart, Forsgren).
- [ ] The prescription names its preconditions (leadership must change the scorecard, Allspaw) and the risks it creates (ungoverned shadow tooling, Fournier; rubber-stamp renewals, Dekker).
- [ ] Adversaries, when they appear, change the mechanism. A closing sentence that just adds "and attackers exploit the gap" is a bolt-on (Woods and Dekker, round 1).
- [ ] Contested claims stay contested. Do not state as settled what the page's limits call contested (essential versus accidental complexity, Brooks).

**Sameness**
- [ ] The opener differs from other pages ("X's thesis is that" appeared three times in one batch).
- [ ] The closer differs from other pages ("becomes the default" echoed Komoroske on Fournier).
- [ ] The Lens position and the angle are not already used elsewhere on the site. Run `bun scripts/lens-index.ts <slug>`, which prints the positions this page argues through and the other pages holding each one. Read those extensions before drafting; that set is what the page has to be novel against, and it is far smaller than the roster. Run the script with no argument for the whole index, where a position held three or more times is flagged as crowded.
  This replaced a hand-kept list on 2026-09-20. The list had drifted inside a single pipeline run: it recorded Fowler at P:7 only when he also holds P:3 and P:5, and Larson at P:8 only when he also holds P:3. The understated P:5 count is why a three-way collision reached a shipped page through three review rounds. A roster list maintained by hand rots faster than it is read.
- [ ] A term repeated many times on one page reads as machine prose even when it is the right word (Dalio's five uses of "operationalization"). A whole proposition repeats the same way and is harder to see: on Cunningham the archived-repository finding appears near-verbatim in a frame gloss, in `limits[4]` and in `live_edge.summary`, and the same aphorism is made twice ("A word that needs a guard rail is doing work its author no longer controls" in `why_they_matter_as_a_thinker`, "Vocabulary that needs a warning label has stopped belonging to its author" in `reading`). One field should carry a point; the others should cite it or drop it.

**Process**
- [ ] Re-read `return-edges.md` against the FINAL page before applying it. The plan is written before the Reading Protocol and the review rounds, applied at Ship after them, and does not move while the page does. On the Anderson run round 3 struck "doctoral student" from five fields and the Willison return-edge label still carried it a day later. This is structural rather than a slip: return edges are written before the page is finished and applied after.
- [ ] A shipped `critiques` edge is the most error-prone thing on a page. Three of three failed the record on Forsgren: Dan North's only durable artifact defends her by name, Beck's never mentions her, and no DHH artifact exists at all. Beck's own page had said so since the day both shipped, so two pages contradicted each other in the open and nothing noticed until a ledger was built.
- [ ] A page with no `facts.md` has never had a single factual field traced to a source. Forsgren's recheck had to source all 33 and found four defects beyond the one it was sent for, including two wrong biographical claims and a 404 on a `source_link` used by two frames.
- [ ] Run `bun scripts/inbound-claims.ts <slug-or-name>` at stage 1 and reconcile every line before drafting. It prints what the roster already asserts about this person, which is the view that would have caught the Beck and Forsgren contradiction at the moment the claim was made rather than months later. All three of the next batch arrive carrying live claims: Humble with five frame credits across Forsgren and Fowler, Goodside with an antecedent edge and a named mention in Willison's reading, Goedecke with a `critiques` edge on Larson and a mention inside one of Larson's limits.
- [ ] End a run with `git status` clean and every branch it created either merged or carrying an open merge request. **Promoted from "Caught once" on 2026-09-22, second sighting.** First: a fix for a recurring failure sat in a working tree while the calibration entry describing that failure was being written. Second: `scripts/run-cost.ts` sat on an unmerged branch with no merge request while the save point told the next session to run it, and the next session got `error: Module not found`. Unmerged work is invisible work, and a save point that cites it is worse than one that does not, because it reads as verified.

**Research**
- [ ] Every fact in the candidate issue is re-verified. The Fournier case had three errors; the Saxe case had six, including every frame it named.
- [ ] Before convening the inclusion Council, run the uptake check: for each candidate frame, its first date and any use by other practitioners. If every frame is under two years old with no outside use, bring it to Julie first instead of spending five seats (Saxe: research already showed this, and the Council went 5 to 0 against).
- [ ] Verify every fact a Council seat asserts before it reaches the page. Seats have been wrong: Saxe seat 2 called a disclosed interest "undisclosed"; Hickey seat 2 proposed Kyle Mitchell as a critic when his essay is sympathetic; Hickey seat 1 quoted uptake evidence with no source. Since 2026-09-17 the fact ledger enforces this structurally, because a seat report is a research request and never a fact.
- [ ] Ask `bun scripts/review-policy.ts <slug>` for the round count instead of recalling it. The three-round rule for `mode-a` lived only in SCHEMA section 7 and two pages shipped with one round.
- [ ] Check the opening of every talk or essay the page cites for a credited premise. "Simple Made Easy" opens by quoting Dijkstra, which changes the attribution of the frame built on it.
- [ ] Anything the draft adds beyond `sources.md` (URLs, essay dates, what an essay argues) gets verified and appended. The first Fournier draft embellished what "The Manager as Debugger" argues.
- [ ] On a pipeline run, confirm the seat reports are on disk under `_working/_council/<date>/` before treating the transcript as a transcript. Caught on Larson and again on Willison: the workflow held verdicts in memory and wrote nothing until Ship, so rounds 2 and 3 answered a verdict list while their briefs told them to quote seats by number. Fixed on 2026-09-17 and not yet exercised, so check rather than assume.

**Return edges**

The first two items were caught twice, on Larson and again on Willison. The last five entered on a single catch each, because they are facts about how the tooling behaves rather than judgment calls waiting on a second sighting, and two of them broke a build and a ledger on 2026-09-17. Julie can send any of them back to "Caught once" if the group is carrying more than it earns.

One thing explains why this group keeps growing. Return-edge labels are the only reader-facing prose on the site that no review round ever sees: they are drafted at stage 4, applied at stage 7, and every other sentence that reaches a reader passes five seats at least once. Every item here is a hand check standing in for a review that does not happen.

- [ ] When the seven-neighbour cap forces a demotion, look first for the edge whose claim is already carried in full by a frame's `one_line_gloss` and `co_originators`. Collapsing that edge into the deep tier costs the page nothing, and it was the right answer three times (Patrick Kua on Fournier, Rudolf Kálmán on Majors twice over).
- [ ] When no candidate for demotion is redundant, rank them by what each edge carries that appears nowhere else on the page, then put the choice to Julie as a decision rather than a note. Fowler's demotion came down to Kent Beck, Ward Cunningham and Eric Evans, all three load-bearing, and it went through as a footnote.
- [ ] Count every on-site neighbour as foreground when planning return edges. `orderNeighbors` in `src/lib/checks.ts` ignores `render_tier` on a neighbour that has a page here, so a file that looks under the cap can be at it in the build. The Larson plan counted Majors at six and the build counted seven.
- [ ] Append a return edge at the end of `adjacent_thinkers`, or renumber `page-claims.md` in the same edit. Claims lines address edges positionally, so an edge inserted mid-list shifts every later index and either fails the ledger or passes against the wrong source.
- [ ] Write a return-edge label from the neighbour's side, opening with what the new thinker adds, then check it against the new page's label for the same pair. Three labels drafted in one pass restated the new page's own wording almost sentence for sentence. **Third sighting on 2026-09-22, with the item followed.** The Humble label on Allspaw opened from the neighbour's side and its middle sentence was still word for word a sentence in the Humble page's own Allspaw label. Opening from the neighbour's side fixes the first sentence and leaves the evidence sentence free to repeat, so the checkable form is stricter: **no sentence in a return-edge label may appear on the new page in the same or near-same words.** Read the two labels side by side, sentence against sentence.
- [ ] **Before widening a neighbour's edge label, open that neighbour's `page-claims.md` line for the edge and check every new clause against the lines it cites.** A `parallel` edge carries no required ledger line, which makes it unchecked rather than uncontroversial, and no gate will notice. On 2026-09-22 the plan widened Beck's Cunningham label from one shared artifact to three on the stated grounds that the facts were already sourced on Beck's own page; Beck's claims line cited `[S:16]` alone, and his ledger held no line for the 1987 pattern-languages report or for the Smalltalk session behind the simplest-thing question. One catch, entered here under this group's exception for facts about how the tooling behaves.
- [ ] Record whether a neighbour's fact ledger already exits 1 before editing it. `rich-hickey` and `martin-fowler` fail on `main` because those legacy pages have no `facts.md`, so running the ledger after an edit says nothing. A page with no ledger can take a `parallel` return edge, which adds no required line, and not a `critiques` or `antecedent` one, which does.

**Context is a budget, and batching spends it (2026-09-20).** A review seat reads every page in the batch plus each one's working set plus every earlier seat report. Batching saves agents, because five seats cover one candidate or three at the same price, but it multiplies what each seat must read. Both halves of that were pushed at once and the late rounds stalled: by round 2 a seat faced roughly half a megabyte before it could write a word, and every agent from round 2 onward died on the 180 second watchdog.

Two causes, both self-inflicted. The instruction to write a seat report to disk said to write the full report and not a summary, which seats read as licence to expand past the brief's 230-word cap; round 1 produced files of 12 to 27KB, and those became required reading for the next round. And reviewers were told to read `sources.md`, the prose research record, which is the largest file in a working set and which `facts.md` plus `page-claims.md` already cover in the form a reviewer needs.

Before adding anything to what a seat reads, price it. Agent count is the cheap axis; per-agent context is the one that fails hard, and it fails as a stall rather than an error.

## Caught once

Patterns with one sighting. They are here so the second sighting is recognisable rather than novel. Move an item up to the checklist when it happens again, and delete it if a run demonstrates it was specific to one page.
