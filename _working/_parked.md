# Parked and rejected candidates

Why this file exists. A park decision used to live only in a comment on a GitLab issue, which is
prose describing a convention rather than a record anything can query. The date sat inside
human-written text, so nothing could compute which parks were due, and a decision recorded that way
rots exactly the way the hand-kept Lens list did inside a single run. Saxe was rejected with
"recheck September 2027" and nothing in the system would ever have surfaced that date.

One line per parked slug, read by `scripts/staleness.ts`:

    - <slug> | <why it was parked> | recheck <YYYY-MM-DD> | attempts <n>

`attempts` is the give-up rule. A candidate parked three times is not a timing problem, and the
fourth recheck should not be scheduled automatically; bring it to Julie or close it for good.

Re-proposing a parked candidate means addressing what was said last time, not simply asking again.
The inclusion Council that rejected it is in the transcript, and a fresh case that ignores those
findings is the same case.

## Named by a Council and deliberately not filed

Not a park and not a rejection. These are people a Council named once, where filing a candidate issue would put a name in the queue that the naming itself argues against. Recorded so a later Council does not spend five people rediscovering the same answer, and so the judgment is not lost the way the pre-2026-09-20 park decisions were. Nothing here is machine-read; re-propose any of them by addressing the line below rather than by asking again.

- **Bill Venners** (Ward Cunningham inclusion Council, 2026-09-22). Named in order to be ruled out. The five-part Artima series he conducted is the best first-person source in the whole Cunningham research record, and an interviewer distributes vocabulary rather than originating it. The same reasoning would rule out most interviewers, which is the point of writing it down once.
- **Bo Leuf** (Ward Cunningham inclusion Council, 2026-09-22). Named for completeness rather than as a case. First author of *The Wiki Way* (2001), which the Cunningham page has to describe accurately for that reason. No independent body of work and no outside use was gathered, and none was looked for.
- **James Shore** (Ward Cunningham inclusion Council, 2026-09-22). Named once. The only claim on record about him is that the Fit project site lists him as its coordinator, a role the Cunningham page explicitly distinguishes from creating the tool. He may well have a corpus of his own, and nothing here has looked at it. Filed under the same rule that kept Gergely Orosz and Jason Yip out of the queue on 2026-09-21: named once, with the naming framed as worth a look rather than as a case. Orosz has since been named by a second Council and is now issue #46, which is how this list is meant to work.
- **Mike Orzen** (Gene Kim inclusion Council, 2026-09-24). Named for completeness and explicitly not recommended. The 2012 post introducing the Three Ways calls him a "fellow co-author" and he appears on no published byline, which the Kim page carries as a limit, quoting the sentence and refusing to print the likeliest reading of it. The reviewer's own words were that he probably fails the filter and that the page needs his sentence more than the site needs his page, which is the whole judgment and is why it is here rather than in the queue.
- **Charles Packer and the MemGPT authors** (Andrej Karpathy inclusion Council, 2026-09-24). Named as a live antecedent question rather than as a case for a page: "Towards LLMs as Operating Systems," submitted 2023-10-12, carries an explicit operating-system metaphor, which bears on the LLM OS framing often credited to Karpathy. The artifact was never opened, so everything about their standing is unknown, and the reviewer said so. The useful residue is already on the record: it is the reason LLM OS stays off the Karpathy page. Re-propose by opening the paper, not by naming it again.
- **Ralph Johnson** (Christopher Alexander inclusion Council, 2026-09-24). Named once, on the argument that he is the pattern movement's most durable academic voice. Recorded here rather than filed separately because he is one of the four *Design Patterns* authors and the collective question belongs in one place, which is issue #51 for Erich Gamma. If the answer there is that the site wants a person rather than a concept, Johnson is the named alternative to Gamma and this line is where that came from.
- **Lorin Hochstein** (Erik Hollnagel review, 2026-09-26). **Filed as #87 on 2026-09-28, on his second naming** (one reviewer in the Richard Cook run, who marked his uptake record as unresearched). The line below is kept for the trail. Named once, by one reviewer, as the software practitioner who carries Safety-II into availability work by name. That much is on the Hollnagel source record at `[S:28]` (his 2026-04-26 post "The normal work of creating reliability"). The Hollnagel research notes pair him with Fred Hebert as the two practitioners who use Hollnagel most directly. Nothing has looked at whether he has vocabulary of his own, as against carrying Hollnagel's, which is the question a candidate issue would have to open on. Kept out of the queue under the same rule as Orosz, Yip and Shore: named once, with a reason that makes him worth a look and no case yet.
- **Phil Venables** (Kelly Shortridge review, 2026-09-28). Named once, in one of five reviewer lists, as a peer-executive voice on the same resilience and security-economics ground, with his long-running essay corpus given as the reason and nothing about it verified. The other four lists named Sounil Yu, Adam Shostack, Josiah Dykstra and Aaron Rinehart, and three of those are now issues #83 to #85 (Rinehart was already #70). Kept out of the queue under the same rule as Orosz, Yip, Shore and Hochstein. One thing sets him apart from those four: Julie's own May 2026 doctrine work already read him beside Shortridge and Yu, so the second naming this rule waits for may be hers to make rather than a Council's.
- **Paul Hammant** (Dave Farley inclusion Council, 2026-10-09). Named once, by the grounding reviewer, as the person commonly credited with documenting trunk-based development, the practice behind Farley's argument against pull requests and Mark Seemann's critique of it. The reviewer marked the whole claim unverified, and nothing about him is on any source record here. Kept out of the queue under the same rule as Orosz, Yip, Shore and Venables: named once, worth a look, no case yet.
- **Gojko Adzic** (Dave Farley inclusion Council, 2026-10-09). Named once, by the novelty and byline reviewer, on the suggestion that *Specification by Example* (2011) is the nearest neighbour to the four-layer acceptance tests on the Farley page and to Dan North's behaviour-driven development. Unverified as given, book date included. If a later Council names him again, the open question is whether executable specification is his vocabulary or a shared one, since the Farley and Terhorst-North pages both sit on it.
