- [S:1] Shostack's own threat modeling resource page introduces the frame as "Most modern threat modeling approaches follow Shostack's Four Question Framework" and states the questions as "What are we working on?", "What can go wrong?", "What are we going to do about it?", "Did we do a good job?". The same page says "Adam published his threat modeling book in 2014." | https://shostack.org/resources/threat-modeling | 2026-10-18
- [S:2] Threat Modeling: Designing for Security, by Adam Shostack, Wiley, 2014; reviewed by Richard Austin in IEEE Cipher (review dated 2014-07-17, issue E121 dated 2014-07-22). | https://www.ieee-security.org/Cipher/BookReviews/2014/Shostak_by_austin.html | 2026-10-18
- [S:3] Shostack's 4QuestionFrame repository asks that the frame be called "Shostack's Four Question Frame for Threat Modeling" or "Shostack's Four Question Framework", says "If you need a license, please treat it as CC-BY", says the questions were originally listed in Threat Modeling: Designing for Security, and lists changes since the book: "we" for "you", "are" for "should" in question 3, "working on" for "building" in question 1, and a habit of asking whether the team did a good "enough" job. | https://github.com/adamshostack/4QuestionFrame | 2026-10-18
- [S:4] Shostack's white paper on the frame (PDF created 2024-05-24 per its metadata) says "Crafting these specific questions was an iterative effort between 2006 and 2014", that "Modern versions first appeared in Threat Modeling: Designing for Security and have been largely consistent since then", and that the framework "is not important enough to be mentioned in a 2010 Blackhat talk or a 2012 3GSE paper." It names predecessors including Hernan (2006), Shostack (2008b), a five-stage process in his July 2008 MSDN article, and a "four-step process" in a 2008 Toorcon talk, and places them inside Microsoft threat modeling work "from roughly 1999 to 2012, including Kohnfelder and Garg (1999) and Swiderski and Snyder (2002)." | https://shostack.org/files/papers/The_Four_Question_Framework.pdf | 2026-10-18
