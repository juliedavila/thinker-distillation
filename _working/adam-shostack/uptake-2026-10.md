## Antecedents

- STRIDE is Kohnfelder and Garg, "The Threats to our Products," Microsoft Interface, 1999-04-01. Shostack's own 20-year retrospective says so and claims nothing for himself [S:8]. Any page frame on STRIDE is `extended` at most, with Kohnfelder and Garg in `co_originators`.
- Threat modeling as a practice predates him inside Microsoft: Swiderski and Snyder, *Threat Modeling* (Microsoft Press, 2004), and the 1999 to 2012 Microsoft body of work he lists himself [S:4].
- The four questions are his by his own account and by the attribution he requests [S:3], with a self-documented 2006 to 2014 evolution [S:4]. The Manifesto restates them with "good enough" and fifteen co-authors [S:5], which is the main reason outside users drift from crediting him to crediting the Manifesto [S:7].

## Verdict: CLEARS

The four questions are vocabulary security teams use by name today, eleven-plus years after first print, with an outside vendor crediting him in those words [S:6] and OWASP using the questions verbatim [S:7]. The issue's own question, whether threat modeling is his or a practice he wrote the standard book on, resolves cleanly. The four-question framing is his; the practice belongs to the Microsoft body of work and STRIDE to Kohnfelder and Garg.

**Weakest clause:** credit drift. The most widely read outside use (OWASP) attributes the questions to the Manifesto [S:7], and the Manifesto is a fifteen-author document [S:5]. A page has to file the frame as his original with the Manifesto as a co-authored restatement, and the outside-use evidence that names him personally is so far one vendor page. One more by-name practitioner use, read at source, would firm this up before the Council.
