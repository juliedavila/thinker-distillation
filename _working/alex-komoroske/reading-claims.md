# Reading claims: Alex Komoroske

Protocol v1. One line per sentence of `reading` and `extension`, each with the grounding-pack IDs it rests on.

## reading

- Komoroske's central claim is that coordination has a cost most organizations never see: every decision depends on agreements, and the drag of those agreements grows faster than headcount. [F:coordination-headwind] [T:thesis]
- Brooks named the same cost for software teams in 1975, and Komoroske extends it to all collaborative work. [E:in:fred-brooks] [S:coordination-headwind:fred-brooks]
- His answer is a change of leadership stance, from architect to gardener, in which the leader sets conditions that make good local choices the obvious ones. [F:gardener-vs-architect] [F:schelling-point-organizations] [T:thesis]
- The heroics smell is the diagnostic that goes with it: a system that needs heroes to keep working is already broken. [F:heroics-smell]
- The frame earns its place by making costs an organization was paying invisibly show up where they can be counted. [T:still-load-bearing]

## extension

- Security is where the gardening stance meets its hardest limit, because a garden assumes nothing in it is working against the gardener. [L:2]
- A security organization can hold both stances by splitting them by audience. [L:2]
- Toward its own engineers it gardens: the secure path becomes the default nobody has to defend choosing, and each new control is judged by how many agreements it adds. [P:2] [F:schelling-point-organizations] [F:coordination-headwind]
- Toward adversaries it architects, with boundaries enforced by infrastructure that hold whether or not anyone converges on them. [P:3] [L:2]
- The split has a weak seam, because an adversary can pass as a good-faith insider, so the enforced boundaries have to hold against insiders as well. [L:2] [P:3]
