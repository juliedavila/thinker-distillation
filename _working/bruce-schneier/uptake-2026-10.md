## Antecedents

- Attack trees are reliability engineering moved to security: fault tree analysis from 1961, Weiss's threat logic trees (1991), Amoroso (1994), and the name itself in Salter et al. 1998 [S:9]. Schneier may be a co-author of Salter et al., which is search-summary only [S:10]. The frame is `extended` at best, with Weiss and Amoroso as originators of the method.
- Schneier's Law restates Babbage (1864) and Kahn, and Schneier says so himself [S:7]. It is an eponym, so it cannot be filed as his original.
- Security theater has no earlier holder in the two sources checked [S:1, S:2]. Both are secondary; the Beyond Fear page itself was not read.

## Verdict: CLEARS

Security theater is the strongest frame in this batch on the test that matters: twenty-two years old, used by name at the CISO level in 2025, and used by the head of the TSA, the agency it was coined against, in 2007 [S:2, S:4]. Security-as-process is also in daily use, though this pass did not document outside uses.

**Weakest clause:** the frame travels without its author. The 2025 CISO use never names him [S:4], so the page would be crediting a phrase that has become field vocabulary, and the only by-name outside credit read at source is the 2004 Forno line [S:2]. The page must also resist claiming attack trees or Schneier's Law as original [S:7, S:9]. Corpus breadth remains the inclusion risk the issue named; this check does not resolve it.
