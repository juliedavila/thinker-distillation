- [S:2] C. S. Holling, "Engineering Resilience versus Ecological Resilience," in *Engineering Within Ecological Constraints* (National Academy of Engineering, National Academies Press, 1996), pp. 31-44, DOI 10.17226/4919. Engineering resilience concentrates on stability "near an equilibrium steady state," measured by "resistance to disturbance and speed of return to the equilibrium." Ecological resilience emphasizes "conditions far from any equilibrium steady state," measured by "the magnitude of disturbance that can be absorbed before the system changes its structure" (p. 33). Quotations via a fetch summary of the NAP reader page. | https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/4919/chapter/4 | 2026-10-18
- [S:3] Panarchy: Lance H. Gunderson and C. S. Holling (eds.), *Panarchy: Understanding Transformations in Human and Natural Systems*, Island Press, 2002. Rocha et al., "Panarchy: ripples of a boundary concept" (arXiv:2012.14312, submitted 2020-12-28), states "In 2002 Holling and colleagues proposed the concept of Panarchy," presenting social-ecological systems as interacting adaptive cycles, and that the adaptive cycle is the feature of panarchy that has drawn the most attention. The book itself was not opened. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.14312 | 2026-10-18

- [S:6] Outside use, security: Kelly Shortridge, "The Red Pill of Resilience in InfoSec" (her keynote text, posted 2018-08-01 per the Shortridge ledger [S:14 there]). "The first application of resilience through the lens of complex systems was by C.S. Holling in 1973 ... Ecological resilience, he said, is measured by the amount of change that could be absorbed before the system's underlying structure changes," citing "Holling, C. S. (1996). Engineering resilience versus ecological resilience." She applies the distinction to defence: "robustness proved dominant in defensive strategy, and can be linked to the concept of engineering resilience," which she calls "a mistake from the evolutionary resilience perspective", and "An engineering-only focus leads to the current challenge of companies needing to constantly stay up to date on patches." This is the engineering versus ecological distinction used by name in security. | https://kellyshortridge.com/blog/posts/red-pill-of-resilience-infosec/ | 2026-10-18
