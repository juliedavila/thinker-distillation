## Corrections to the issue

The issue dates the engineering versus ecological line to 1973. The 1973 paper introduced resilience into ecology; the named distinction, with "engineering resilience" as the term, is the 1996 essay [S:2]. Panarchy is co-authored with Gunderson, as the issue says, and the adaptive cycle is Holling's own and earlier [S:3, S:4].

## Antecedents found

The word resilience has a pre-ecology history in engineering and materials, traced by Alexander (2013), which Shortridge cites [S:10]. Holling's own 1996 essay names the older equilibrium sense as engineering resilience, so he credits it by naming it [S:2]. Gunderson's "adaptive capacity" (2000) builds on Holling's 1973 paper, per the Woods ledger, so it descends from his work.

## Read

Fifty years of work on one problem: how systems absorb change without losing their structure. The two-year clause is not in question. The vocabulary clears the non-software wording on the security side: Shortridge uses the engineering versus ecological distinction by name and cites the 1996 essay, and Geraghty carries the adaptive cycle and panarchy into team and organisational work. The gap is software reliability. The resilience engineering writing I checked (Hebert on Woods) does not cite him [S:11], so his line into software runs through Shortridge and the security writers, and much less through the SRE tradition.
