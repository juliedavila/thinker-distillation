## Named frames verified in primary text (Platform Engineering excerpt, O'Reilly/Coder PDF)

Source: [excerpt PDF](https://www.datocms-assets.com/19109/1730410983-platform-eng-excerpt.pdf)

- **Platform** (adopted, not coined): "We use Evan Bottcher's definition from 2018... A platform is a foundation of self-service APIs, tools, services, knowledge, and support that are arranged as a compelling internal product." Attribution: Bottcher.
- **Platform engineering**: "the discipline of developing and operating platforms. The goal of this discipline is to manage overall system complexity in order to deliver leverage to the business."
- **Leverage**: "the work of a few engineers on a platform team reduces the work of the greater organization."
- **Platform as a product**: "it is essential to view a platform as a product... requires more than just performatively hiring product managers."
- **Glue**: "our term for the integration code, one-off automation, configuration, and management tools."
- **Over-general swamp**: "forms as the glue spreads. Each application team makes independent choices across the array of primitives."
- **How platform engineering clears the swamp** (four moves): limiting primitives while minimizing overhead; reducing per-application glue; centralizing the cost of migrations; allowing application developers to operate what they develop.
- **Standardization via authority isn't enough**: curate primitives through a product approach instead of appeals to authority.
- **Shadow platforms**: "duplicative platforms that application engineering teams sometimes build for themselves... The goal is not to restrict all of it, but to be aware of it."
- Chapter heading: "Managing the Accidental Complexity of Human Coordination" (explicit Brooks vocabulary).

## Critics and counterpositions

- **Charity Majors**, "Engineering Management: The Pendulum Or The Ladder" (2019) and "The Engineer/Manager Pendulum" (2017): argues against treating management as a ladder to climb; the best leaders move back and forth. A counterposition to the ladder framing, not a critique of Fournier by name. [charity.wtf](https://charity.wtf/2019/01/04/engineering-management-the-pendulum-or-the-ladder/)
- **Uri Bar-Joseph**, "On the merits of READMEs. A response to Camille Fournier." Direct named rebuttal. [Medium](https://medium.com/@uribarjoseph/on-the-merits-of-readmes-a-response-to-camille-fournier-1b8ac9d7119e)
- **Ed Batista**, "To README or Not to README?" (2021): engages Fournier's critique. [edbatista.com](https://edbatista.com/2021/03/to-readme-or-not-to-readme.html)
- Common review limitation of *The Manager's Path*: broad overview, shallow on any single topic. [InfoQ review](https://www.infoq.com/articles/book-review-managers-path)
- Industry-level critique of platform engineering as rebranded DevOps / ops teams renamed (not aimed at Fournier; the book itself argues against this failure mode).

## Antecedent check (post-Council)

- **Tech lead as a role, not a rung.** Patrick Kua's *Talking with Tech Leads* (Leanpub, about 2014) predates *The Manager's Path* (2017) and states "Tech lead is not a promotion. It is a role change." Fournier's framing extends an existing idea; attribute as `extended` with Kua as co-originator. [Thoughtworks](https://www.thoughtworks.com/en-us/insights/books/talking-with-tech-leads), [InfoQ review](https://www.infoq.com/articles/book-review-talking-tech-leads/)
