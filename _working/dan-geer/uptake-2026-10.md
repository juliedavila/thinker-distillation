## Antecedents

- The diversity argument for computing, drawn from biology, is in Forrest, Somayaji and Ackley, HotOS 1997 [S:2]. The agricultural and biological term is a century older, as the issue says. What the 2003 paper adds is the application to a single vendor's market dominance as a national risk. That application is co-authored with six others, Schneier among them [S:1], so the frame is `co-authored` and `extended` from Forrest et al.
- The 2014 bill-of-materials line [S:3] predates the SBOM policy wave, but it is one clause in a liability proposal and the antecedent search for SBOM was not run. Do not claim priority.
- Software liability as policy has a long legal literature; not searched in this pass.

## Verdict: BORDERLINE

Monoculture clears on its own: it came back by name after the 2024 CrowdStrike outage, when an ISACA piece credited Geer for it two decades on [S:6]. The 2014 proposals are durable, citable and clearly his alone [S:3, S:4], and they read as current policy (liability shift, SBOM, end-of-life). Still, this pass found coverage of them, not practitioners using them by name.

**Weakest clause:** the one frame that travels carries seven author names, Schneier among them, and extends a 1997 paper [S:1, S:2]. The frames that are his alone (liability safe harbor, buy-all-vulns, abandonment) show no outside use by name in this pass. A Geer page would rest on sole-authored proposals the field quotes rarely and a shared frame that the Schneier page would also want. That makes it a roster-shape decision to take together with Schneier.
