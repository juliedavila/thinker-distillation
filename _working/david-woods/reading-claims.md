# Reading claims: David Woods

Protocol v1. One line per sentence of `reading` and `extension`, each with the grounding-pack IDs it rests on.

## reading

- Woods describes complex systems as joint cognitive systems, in which people and automation do the thinking together and failures live in the joint between them. [F:joint-cognitive-system] [T:thesis]
- Such systems survive surprises through adaptive capacity: reserves that people and organizational arrangements supply, and that stay invisible until they are needed. [F:adaptive-capacity] [T:thesis]
- The Law of Stretched Systems explains why those reserves shrink: slack looks like inefficiency, so every system gets pushed toward the edge of its envelope, where small disturbances produce large failures. [F:law-of-stretched-systems] [T:working-line]
- Graceful extensibility, the ability to stretch under conditions nobody designed for, is the property resilience engineering aims at. [F:graceful-extensibility]
- The original papers are dense, which is why carriers such as Allspaw brought the vocabulary into software. [L:1] [E:in:john-allspaw]

## extension

- The Law of Stretched Systems predicts what happens to security automation gains: the capacity they free up gets spent on more load. [F:law-of-stretched-systems]
- A security leader who wants resilience has to defend slack as reserve for surprises, and should expect that defense to be contested for as long as the same pressure reads slack as waste. [F:adaptive-capacity] [F:law-of-stretched-systems]
- That reserve is where security behaves as an emergent property: no single control absorbs a novel attack, and what absorbs it is people with the time and context to adapt. [P:2] [F:adaptive-capacity] [F:graceful-extensibility]
- A program that lets its automation savings turn into throughput ends up as stretched as before, and the reserve it never rebuilt is the one an attacker eventually finds. [F:law-of-stretched-systems] [L:2]
