- [S:1] Donald A. Norman, "Categorization of Action Slips," Psychological Review, Volume 88, Number 1, January 1981 (copyright 1981 APA, 0033-295X/81/8801-0001), University of California, San Diego. Abstract opens: "A slip is the error that occurs when a person does an action that is not intended." Three major categories: errors in the formation of the intention (mode and description errors), faulty activation of schemas (capture errors and others), and faulty triggering. Credits Meringer's collections and calls Freud's the "best early theoretical account". Abstract: slips "are defined to be situations in which the user's intention was proper, but the results did not conform to that intention", and the paper derives design principles from one class of errors, slips of action. - [S:5] Edwin L. Hutchins, James D. Hollan and Donald A. Norman, "Direct Manipulation Interfaces," Human-Computer Interaction, 1985, Volume 1, pp. 311-338, Lawrence Erlbaum. Section headings "The Gulf of Execution" and "The Gulf of Evaluation": "The gulf of execution is bridged by making the commands and mechanisms of the system match the thoughts and goals of the user"; "The gulf of evaluation is bridged by making the output displays present a good conceptual model of the system that is readily perceived, interpreted, and evaluated." A version also appears as a chapter in User Centered System Design: New Perspectives on Human-Computer Interaction (Norman and Draper, 1986). Read from PDF text layer. | https://vis.csail.mit.edu/classes/6.859/readings/pdfs/Hutchins-DirectManipulationInterfaces.pdf | 2026-10-18
- [S:6] Don Norman, "Signifiers, not affordances," jnd.org, dated 2008-11-17, originally ACM Interactions 15(6) (November-December 2008). "When I introduced the term into design in 1988 I was referring to perceivable affordances. Since then, the term has been widely used and misused." The term was "invented by the perceptual psychologist J. J. Gibson to refer to a relationship", and for Gibson affordances "simply existed" whether or not perceivable. A signifier is "some sort of indicator, some signal in the physical or social world that can be interpreted meaningfully." Read via fetch summary. | https://jnd.org/signifiers-not-affordances/ | 2026-10-18
