## Affordance and signifier: the record

"Affordance" originates with J. J. Gibson, and Norman says so himself: the term was "invented by the perceptual psychologist J. J. Gibson to refer to a relationship" (S:6). His move was to carry it into design in 1988 as perceivable affordance, and the 1999 essay already separated real from perceived affordances (S:7). The 2008 "Signifiers, not affordances" is his own correction of how designers used his word: "the term has been widely used and misused", and the perceivable cue is better named a signifier (S:6). He later rejected the reading that he had abandoned affordances (S:8). A page must treat affordance as `extended` from Gibson, with signifier as Norman's own term. The draft already has `derived_from: gibson-affordances`, which matches.

## Antecedents and attribution risks

- **Gulfs are co-authored.** The 1985 paper is Hutchins, Hollan and Norman (S:5). The draft and the Subramonyam abstract say "Norman's gulfs". That is how the field talks, but the frame would be `co-authored` here.
- **Slip versus mistake.** The 1981 paper's text never uses "mistake" (S:2). It defines a slip as an unintended action and builds a taxonomy of slips, credited back through Freud and Meringer (S:1). Reason had published on actions not as planned in 1979 (S:4). The live Reason page already hedges this through a secondary account, and the evidence here supports keeping it hedged. The contrast probably enters Norman's work later (1983 or the 1988 book), but this pass did not find where.
- **Draft source links are dead.** Both jnd.org links in the draft 404 (S:13).

## Uptake in software, security and AI

Affordances are working vocabulary in API and developer-experience writing (S:10). The gulfs are the base model a CHI 2024 paper extends to LLM prompting (S:9). In SRE, slips and mistakes appear in blameless-postmortem practice without his name (S:11). I found no use in security or agent authorization in this bounded pass.
