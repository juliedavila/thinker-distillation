## Antecedents

Cost of delay first appears in co-authored work: the 1991 and 1997 editions with Preston Smith (S:1, S:2). So a cost-of-delay frame is `co-authored` at minimum until a solo pre-1991 Reinertsen article is read. A glossary claim that he coined the term in 2009 is wrong on dates (S:11). The queueing and batch arguments rest on queueing theory and Toyota-style lean manufacturing, which he explicitly applies to design work (S:3). I did not run a separate search for Little's Law or Kingman, and the page should not call the queueing math his. The original contribution is moving these tools into product development economics, and the claim that the optimum batch is economic and need not be minimal (S:5).

## Uptake in software

Strong in agile and lean software management: SAFe's prioritization model is built on his cost-of-delay quote (S:6), CD3/WSJF is used as a scheduling rule (S:8), Ries promoted the Flow book to the startup audience (S:5), and Wake carried design-in-process inventory into agile software management in 2002 (S:4). In DevOps the practice has spread while the attribution has dropped: DORA's "Working in small batches" never names him (S:9). I found no use in security, SRE incident work or AI agent security in this search.
