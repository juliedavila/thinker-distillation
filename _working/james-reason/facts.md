## The Swiss cheese model: origin, co-originators, versions

## Error taxonomy and its antecedents

- [S:28] British Academy memoir: "Jim first identified and labelled crucial distinctions between slips (automatic actions gone wrong), lapses (memory failures), and mistakes (flawed decision-making)." This is a firstness claim in a memoir by colleagues; do not print it in site voice. See [S:29]. | https://www.thebritishacademy.ac.uk/documents/6053/Memoirs-23-04-Reason.pdf | 2026-10-02
- [S:29] Norman's 1981 paper on action slips: D. A. Norman, "Categorization of action slips", *Psychological Review* 88(1): 1-15, January 1981. The Crossref record confirms author, title, venue, volume, issue, pages and date. Its 1981 date precedes Reason and Mycielska (1982) and *Human Error* (1990), which is one reason [S:28]'s "first" stays out of the page; [S:30]'s source also says the slip/mistake distinction "was first made by Norman (1981)". Bounds: the paper's own text was not read (APA PsycNET serves a bot wall); nothing about its contents beyond the title may be cited from this line, and this line does not establish that Norman's slip category predates Reason's own slip work (see [S:52]). Cold reader editor 2026-10-02: the line was headed "Antecedent for the slip category", a priority conclusion the line never supported. | https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1037/0033-295X.88.1.1 | 2026-10-02
- [S:30] Antecedent for GEMS (secondary, David Embrey, Human Reliability Associates): Rasmussen's skill, rule and knowledge classification was published in 1979, 1982 and 1987; Reason's Generic Error Modelling System "is an extension of the SRK approach" and maps skill-based slips, rule-based mistakes and knowledge-based mistakes onto Rasmussen's three levels. The same document says "The distinction between slips and mistakes was first made by Norman (1981). Slips are defined as errors in which the intention is correct, but a failure" occurs in carrying it out. Supports: GEMS is `extended` from Rasmussen, never `original`; and the slip/mistake distinction is credited to Norman, not Reason, by this secondary source. | https://mail.humanreliability.com/articles/Understanding%20Human%20Behaviour%20and%20Error.pdf | 2026-10-02
