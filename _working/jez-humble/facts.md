## Release decoupled from deploy

- [S:7] "Continuous Delivery vs Continuous Deployment," on Humble's own site, 13 August 2010, written in the first person ("Dave and I published our book"). States the distinction the frame rests on in two separate sentences: "There's the somewhat pedantic fact that deployment does not imply release. As we say in the book, you can continuously deploy to UAT - no big deal." And: "What makes continuous deployment special is deploying every change that passes the automated tests (and optionally a short QA gate) _to production_." On the delivery side: "Continuous delivery is about putting the release schedule in the hands of the business, not in the hands of IT," with "Any build could _potentially_ be released to users at the touch of a button using a fully automated process in a matter of seconds or minutes," because "However it doesn't always make sense to release every good build to users." CORRECTED 2026-09-22 during the adversarial audit: the intake line ran the first two sentences together inside one pair of quotation marks joined by an ellipsis, producing a quotation that does not appear at this URL, and it dropped the parenthetical "(and optionally a short QA gate)," which narrows what continuous deployment means. Quote the sentences separately or paraphrase. | https://continuousdelivery.com/2010/08/continuous-delivery-vs-continuous-deployment/ | 2026-09-22
- [S:14] Pete Hodgson, "Feature Toggles (aka Feature Flags)," martinfowler.com, 09 October 2017: "Using Release Toggles in this way is the most common way to implement the Continuous Delivery principle of 'separating [feature] release from [code] deployment.'" ## Critics

- [S:19] Keunwoo Lee, "A review of *Accelerate: The Science of Lean Software and DevOps*," 07 June 2022. Durable artifact on the author's own site. Names Humble repeatedly ("On Twitter, you can see *Accelerate* author J. Humble writing things like this"). Argues against the work on four stated grounds, under his own heading "Here is a summary of the main issues I have": "The things being measured are inadequate: The development performance metrics are incomplete and sometimes circular"; "The instruments used to measure them are inadequate: The survey-based methodology is vulnerable to halo effects and other threats to validity"; "The statistical analysis of the measurements is suspect: The presentation of statistical correlations is sloppy and misleading"; "The published results are impossible to evaluate or replicate: The authors have released neither the full survey questions nor the data gathered from them." Concludes "Nevertheless, the claim to have scientifically validated a set of superior software development practices seems questionable."
