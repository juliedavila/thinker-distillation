## Essay corpus (Substack, April 2025 to September 2026)

Dates are publication dates. Quotes are verbatim.

- **"AI Security Notes 4/10/2025"**: "System level AI security is making more progress than model level security"; discusses Google DeepMind's CaMeL. [link](https://joshuasaxe181906.substack.com/p/ai-security-notes-4102025)
- **"AI security notes 5/8/2025"**: says the core obstacle is ML's weakness at generalizing from public training data to private enterprise contexts and unseen attacker tooling. [link](https://joshuasaxe181906.substack.com/p/ai-security-notes-582025)
- **"AI security notes, 5/30/2025"**: "This might be the highest leverage period for AI security in our generation." [link](https://joshuasaxe181906.substack.com/p/ai-security-notes-5302025)
- **"AI security notes 8/4/25: How do we build agent security frameworks the scaling laws won't force us to rewrite?"** Lists what won't change (actions are sampled, agents live in privilege sandboxes, sandboxes have bugs, agents get monitored) and what will (the set of trusted actions grows, sandboxes grow and get buggier, more agents per human monitor). Calls for SOC practice built around agents: "Agents have different affordances; we can monitor their latent states, chains of thoughts, and their every action. We can pause them and roll them back." [link](https://joshuasaxe181906.substack.com/p/ai-security-notes-8425-how-do-we)
- **"AI security notes 9/15: We can get control of prompt injection without any technical miracles"** (2025-09-16). **Prompt injection vulnerability versus risk.** "A prompt injection vulnerability exists when an agent uses an LLM to take a Dangerous Action … without Human Confirmation … while having Attacker-Controlled Data in its context window … and while users, application owners, or both, haven't explicitly accepted the risk." "A prompt injection risk is a prompt injection vulnerability except that both application owners and users have explicitly chosen to accept it." "Prompt injection vulnerabilities are just traditional procedural code security vulnerabilities," to be found with control- and data-flow analysis in CI/CD. Risks are handled with least privilege plus "probabilistic detection funnels." "As we get better at mitigating risks, we'll accept more of them, and increase AI agent autonomy." [link](https://joshuasaxe181906.substack.com/p/ai-security-notes-915-we-can-get)
- **"A take on how we should approach securing AI agents now and in the long term"** (2025-10-08; a talk given at The Curve and as a keynote at the AI Security Forum). "Agent security is neither traditional security nor pure alignment; it must fuse both—implementing deterministic controls that assume misalignment, while maximizing p(aligned) within as small as possible a trust region." "Start small and deliberately dial up trust as red teaming, evaluations, and real-world use demonstrate reliability." "Don't oversell probabilistic defenses; use them only for intentionally accepted residual risk." [link](https://joshuasaxe181906.substack.com/p/a-take-on-how-we-should-approach)
- **"Swallowing the reality pill in AI agent security"** (2025-12-23). Presents his **three nested layers** (deterministic controls, alignment controls, detection and response) and admits: "I think my security model is still correct, but there's a sense in which that doesn't really matter." Names the **friction paradox**: "the tendency for security controls to get ignored and have no impact as they get more beautiful and academically perfect." Example: Claude Code users "invoke the --dangerously-skip-permissions flag almost universally" (his claim, not independently measured). Prescription: a sandbox where skipping permissions only damages the sandbox; don't write off classifiers ("far better than nothing"); "Asynchronous detection and response is the lowest friction security control ... but the least mature area of AI agent security." Suggests managed detection and response for AI applications. [link](https://joshuasaxe181906.substack.com/p/swallowing-the-reality-pill-in-ai)
- **"On apples, oranges, and classical ML versus LLM security performance"** (2026-01-02). [link](https://joshuasaxe181906.substack.com/p/on-apples-oranges-and-classical-ml)
- **"How to defend an exploding AI attack surface when the attackers haven't shown up (yet)"** (2026-01-06). **AI security risk overhang**: "an exponentially growing attack surface is not being attacked yet," so defenders lack the empirical signal to tune defenses or justify friction. **Dialable controls**: "controls that are lightweight and low friction now but which can be easily dialed to be more severe, and higher-friction, as risks materialize, and as a function of how they materialize." Applied to all three layers. Ends with a call for "a risk heatmap grounded in observed attacker behavior across the industry" and for threat sharing. [link](https://joshuasaxe181906.substack.com/p/how-to-defend-an-exploding-ai-attack)
- **"What to ask vendors at RSA"** (2026-03-23). [link](https://joshuasaxe181906.substack.com/p/what-to-ask-vendors-at-rsa)
- **"Exploits don't cause cyberattacks"** (2026-04-09). "Technologies don't cause attackers to do cyberattacks; rather, attackers do cyberattacks to achieve their desired outcomes in the easiest ways possible in their own social, economic, and political settings." Argues that cheap AI phishing, voice, and deepfake tools did not produce the attack floods predicted. Proposes actor-centric questions: which goals are newly reachable, which attacker groups are unblocked, and what cultural and organizational barriers slow adoption. [link](https://joshuasaxe181906.substack.com/p/exploits-dont-cause-cyberattacks)
- **"Against technocentrism in AI safety"** (2026-05-12). "The central object is people; what they do with models, what others do in response, what incentives shape behavior." Defines technocentrism as a mix of methodological, sociological, funding, and epistemic biases. From Meta content moderation: "attackers didn't use gradients, they used Photoshop." Aviation analogy: safety "emerged from reporting cultures, institutional learning loops, investigation procedures, insurance structures." Proposes a closed working group modeled on FIRST, run under Chatham House rules, producing "the rudiments of an incident science for AI" in a quarterly memo. [link](https://joshuasaxe181906.substack.com/p/against-technocentrism-in-ai-safety)

## Critics and counterpositions

No critic who names Saxe was found. Everything below is a counterposition (a `parallel` edge at most, per calibration).

- **Simon Willison**, ["You can't solve AI security problems with more AI"](https://simonwillison.net/2022/Sep/17/prompt-injection-more-ai/) (September 17, 2022) and restated in 2025: detection-based prompt injection defenses fail, so 99% is a failing grade. This cuts against Saxe's defense of classifiers as "far better than nothing." Saxe's position is qualified: probabilistic defenses only for accepted residual risk.
- **Nasr, Carlini, et al.**, ["The Attacker Moves Second"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09023) (October 2025, 14 or 15 authors from OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google DeepMind): adaptive attacks beat 12 published defenses with over 90% success. This is empirical pressure on the alignment-controls layer. Saxe answers on different ground, arguing that real attackers are not adaptive red teams ("the messy middle of real usage").
- **Frontier-lab capability-threshold policy** (launch gating on dangerous cyber capabilities): the position Saxe argues against in the 2026 policy essays. No single named author; do not make it an edge.
- **Built-in limits the page must carry:**
  - Absence-of-evidence risk: "no prompt injection damages yet" rests on public reporting and a model-assisted Bayesian estimate. He acknowledges under-reporting.
  - Interest: since 2026 he co-founds a company selling AI cyber defense, while arguing against restricting cyber-capable models and for defender adoption. The page should state this plainly.
  - His three-layer model, by his own account, was not adopted by the developers it was designed for.
  - The essay corpus is short and fast-moving, and some positions have already reversed (his prompt injection urgency from 2023–2026).
