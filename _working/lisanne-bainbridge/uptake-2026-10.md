## Antecedents

The paper cites its own antecedents. Wiener and Curry's 1980 "Flight-deck automation: promises and problems" already documented monitoring failures and setup errors around automatic equipment, and she cites Wiener and Curry and Rouse (1981) as writers who had already argued the Fitts-list allocation was insufficient (S:3, S:7). The original contribution is the framing of these as ironies and the compact statement of the easy-parts claim. The deskilling point is hers in this paper, though aviation human factors had related findings in hand.

## Uptake in software, SRE and AI

Uptake is broad and current. Resilience-engineering practitioners read it as a core text (Hebert, S:11), Baxter et al. carried it into cloud computing in 2012 (S:10), and since 2024 it has become a standard lens for generative and agentic AI: a Microsoft Research team's "Ironies of Generative AI" (S:12), a Programming 2024 workshop paper on LLM code synthesis (S:15), and practitioner essays on supervising agents (S:13, S:14). The phrase travels on its own; Simkute et al.'s abstract uses "ironies of automation" without naming her.
