## Named frames: antecedent searches

- [S:17] The antecedent search for "Principles" (numbered, written-down decision rules a person or organization commits to and revises; the page's gloss says "The genre is the contribution"): searched for earlier numbered personal and organizational principle sets and dated Dalio's own document. Result: antecedents exist for the genre. Benjamin Franklin's *Autobiography* sets out thirteen numbered virtues, each with a short written precept, tracked daily on a chart and revised (humility was added after a friend's criticism). Jeffrey Liker's *The Toyota Way: 14 Management Principles from the World's Greatest Manufacturer* (McGraw-Hill, 2004) codifies an organization's operating philosophy as numbered management principles. The page itself names Munger's *Poor Charlie's Almanack* (first edition 2005, ed. Peter Kaufman) as "same form." Dalio's own document is first dated by the 2010 leak (S:13). What appears to be Dalio's is the specific practice of running a firm against a single long, living, published principles document; the genre of numbered written principles predates him. | https://www.excellence-in-literature.com/?p=1964 | 2026-10-15 | re-verified 2026-10-15
- [S:18] OPEX Society bookstore listing: *The Toyota Way: 14 Management Principles from the World's Greatest Manufacturer*, Jeffrey Liker, McGraw-Hill Education, publication year 2004; Liker "shares his insights into the foundational principles at work in the Toyota culture." - [S:19] The antecedent search for "Radical transparency": searched for uses of the exact phrase before Dalio's earliest dated use (2010, S:13). Result: earlier dated uses exist. Wikipedia's article records that "the explicit political argument for 'radical transparency' was first made in a 2001 Foreign Affairs article" (Allen Hammond); that Chris Anderson of Wired (2006-11-26) described a corporate shift to "radical transparency," with the "whole product development process [is] laid bare, and opened to customer input"; that Clive Thompson wrote on it in Wired (March 2007); and that "by 2008, the term was being used to describe the WikiLeaks platform." The same article credits Dalio only with having "wrote about the concept in his 2017 book, Principles." No dated Dalio use earlier than 2010 was found. - [S:20] The antecedent search for "Believability-weighted decision-making": searched for the phrase and for earlier methods that weight judgment by demonstrated track record. Result: no use of the phrase before Dalio was found; secondary sources trace it to his Principles (S:14, S:15). The closest earlier method found is the Classical Model of structured expert judgment. Colson and Cooke, "Expert elicitation: using the classical model to validate experts' judgments" (*Review of Environmental Economics and Policy* 12(1), 2018) says it "scores experts on their performance in assessing the calibration questions and then produces performance-weighted combinations of the experts," and that it had been "used in thirty-three unique applications" from 2006 through March 2015. That is an antecedent for the mechanism in a different field (risk analysis), in use before the 2010 record of Dalio's document, with no evidence Dalio drew on it. The name and its use as an organizational decision rule appear to be Dalio's. | https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/62172 | 2026-10-15 | re-verified 2026-10-15
