Originator sourcing pass, 2026-10-16 to 2026-10-17. The build warned on one frame, `complect`, which was filed `popularized` and named no originator in `co_originators` or `derived_from`. **Complect: what the search found.** The antecedent search [S:50] tested one part of the frame: whether anyone used the word "complect" in a software sense before "Simple Made Easy" (September 2011). It found nobody. Every software result traces the word to the talk. Wiktionary gives two archaic senses and no computing sense ([S:30]), and the talk itself says "I found it" and "It happens to be an archaic word" while crediting no one for the software use ([S:14]). That is option (b): the software meaning is Hickey's, and the word is old English, which the gloss already says ("An archaic English word ... revived for software"). The frame is refiled `original`, the kind this page already gives guardrail programming on the same grounds ([S:36]). The search did not test the underlying idea of entangled program parts. The gloss's closing sentence already names coupling and connascence as older ideas it overlaps, and that sentence stays. The search covered the open web only. Usenet, books and the pre-2011 Clojure mailing list were not searched directly.

**Persistent data structures.** Driscoll, Sarnak, Sleator and Tarjan's 1989 paper ([S:51], read from the PDF) calls itself "a study of persistence in data structures" and sets out "generic techniques for making linked data structures persistent." Its own introduction says other researchers had already built persistent stacks, queues and search trees, so the paper is not the first statement of persistence and the page does not present it as one. Hickey's history cites it and says he looked at its fat node approach after Okasaki and turned away from it because "the in-place updating required to obtain the advertised bounds made them impractical for multithreaded use" ([S:52]). The gloss sentence keeps that scope ("to reach its advertised bounds").

**Cognitect CTO.** A contemporaneous source now supports it: InfoQ's speaker bio for his QCon San Francisco 2013 talk reads "He is the CTO at Cognitect" ([S:53]). The page says only "where Hickey was CTO," with no dates, so the wording is supported as it stands. [S:9]'s note that Wikipedia was the only source naming the title was updated.

**Strange Loop keynote.** The schedule announcement on the conference's Google group (Alex Miller, 2011-06-30) lists "Rich Hickey (Clojure/core)" first of four keynotes, without titles. His only other session in the announcement is "Learn to Play Go," with Jeff Brown ([S:54]). Read with the attendee account that names his keynote "Simple Made Easy" ([S:32]), the claim now rests on two independent sources, and the wording is kept. [S:32] was updated to point at [S:54].
