## Added at drafting (stage 3, 2026-09-28)

- [S:41] Cook & Woods (1994) credit the pair to Reason in their own chapter: practitioners "work at what Reason calls the 'sharp end' of the system (Reason, 1990; Fig. 13.1)", and "Those at the 'blunt end' of the system, to continue Reason's analogy, affect safety through their effect on the constraints and resources acting on the practitioners at the sharp end." Settles the inclusion Council's open question on direction: Reason's use (1990) precedes the 1994 chapter, and the chapter says so. Text read from the PDF of "Operating at the Sharp End: The Complexity of Human Error", pp. 255-256. | https://securityandtechnology.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/operatingatthesharp-2.pdf | 2026-09-28
- [S:42] The antecedent search for How Complex Systems Fail. Method: read the text's opening and its reference list ([S:11]: it opens on proposition 1 with no credited premise, and its reading list is Cook's and Woods's own work plus Cook's 1999 piece); one web search on 2026-09-28 pairing the title with Perrow's *Normal Accidents* (1984), as a positive control for reaching earlier work on complex-system failure. Result: the search returned Perrow's 1984 book, which argues that accidents are normal in complex, tightly coupled systems, and later commentaries that place Cook after Perrow and Reason; it found no earlier text setting out these propositions as a list, and no source crediting the list to anyone else. Cook's text does not cite Perrow ([S:11]). Its vocabulary is borrowed in places: "latent failures" in proposition 4 is Reason's ([S:13]), "sharp end" in proposition 11 is Reason's by Cook's own 1994 account ([S:41]), and proposition 8's "hindsight bias" is a term the text uses without claiming it. So `original` holds for the list as a text and for no single proposition's idea. | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_Accidents | 2026-09-28
- [S:43] The eighteen proposition headings, as published, include proposition 2 "Complex systems are heavily and successfully defended against failure", proposition 3 "Catastrophe requires multiple failures – single point failures are not enough.", proposition 4 "Complex systems contain changing mixtures of failures latent within them.", proposition 5 "Complex systems run in degraded mode" (whose text says "complex systems run as broken systems"), proposition 8 "Hindsight biases post-accident assessments of human performance", proposition 11 "Actions at the sharp end resolve all ambiguity", proposition 16 "Safety is a characteristic of systems and not of their components" and proposition 17 "People continuously create safety." The text does not use the term "blunt end". | https://how.complexsystems.fail/ | 2026-09-28
- [S:44] "Distancing through differencing" as Cook and Woods define it: "By focusing on the differences, they see no lessons for their own operation and practices or only narrow well bounded responses. We call this pattern-distancing through differencing." The case is a chemical fire in the clean room of a high-technology manufacturing plant, where staff set aside a similar earlier fire at the company's overseas facility on the grounds of differences in location, equipment and safety systems.
