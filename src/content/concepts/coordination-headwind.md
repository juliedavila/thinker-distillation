display_name: Coordination Headwind
one_line_gloss: >
  The invisible drag on collaborative work: the cost of every agreement a
  decision depends on. Grows superlinearly with team size and absorbs
  effort before any of it reaches the work.

limits:
  - headline: It cannot tell you which coordination to cut
    severity: structural
    elaboration: >
      The frame diagnoses *that* coordination has a cost; it does not adjudicate
      which coordination links are load-bearing (preventing real failure) and
      which are headwind (preventing nothing). Without a complement (value-stream
      mapping, blast-radius reasoning, organizational risk register), the frame
      can talk an organization into cutting the wrong agreements and discovering
      the cost the hard way.
  - headline: It is silent on coordination's productive forms
    severity: local
    elaboration: >
      Coordination is also how shared understanding is built, how culture
      transmits, how new hires absorb the model the system runs on. The
      headwind framing names the friction without naming the conditions under
      which coordination is the work. A team that internalizes only the
      headwind reading risks pathologically minimizing the interactions that
      were never costs to begin with.
  - headline: The superlinear curve is asserted, not measured
    severity: local
    elaboration: >
      Brooks's original observation came from project debriefs, not
      instrumentation. Komoroske's generalization extends the intuition across
      domains without per-domain empirical grounding. The frame's predictive
      power is qualitative; using it as a quantitative argument for any
      specific team size threshold is overreach.

still_load_bearing_despite: >
  These limits are limits *on application*, not on the frame itself. The
  underlying claim, that coordination cost grows with the number of
  agreements a decision depends on, and that this cost is mostly invisible to
  the people inside it, survives every challenge above. The frame's value is
  diagnostic: it names a force that would otherwise be felt as "things just
  feel hard." A diagnostic frame doesn't need to be prescriptive to be
  load-bearing.
